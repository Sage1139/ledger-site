Quick answers

The questions
everyone asks.

What am I actually buying when I buy a share?

A tiny piece of ownership in a real company. If a company is split into 100 crore shares and you hold 10, you own 10 of those 100 crore pieces. See it in the Stocks lesson →

Who am I buying from?

Another person or institution who wants to sell at that moment. The exchange (NSE or BSE) simply matches buyers and sellers. Try the order book →

Why do prices go up and down?

Every trade happens at a price both sides agree on. When more people want to buy than sell, buyers have to offer more, and the price rises. When sellers outnumber buyers, it falls. Play with supply and demand →

How do I make money from it?

Two ways: the price rises above what you paid (capital gain), and the company shares profits with you (dividends). Mutual funds do the same thing for a basket of companies. See how returns work →

Where are my shares kept?

In your demat account, held at a depository (NSDL or CDSL), not with your broker. Learn the three accounts →

Should I start with F&O?

Almost certainly not. SEBI's own study found that nearly 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lost money in FY2025-26. Learn it here, practise it in the simulator, and decide later. Read the F&O lesson →
