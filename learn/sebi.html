CHAPTER 1

SEBI: the market's
referee.

SEBI stands for the Securities and Exchange Board of India. It was set up in 1988 and given legal powers by the SEBI Act of 1992. Think of it as the referee of the market: it doesn't play, but it makes the rules and punishes anyone who breaks them.

🛡️Protects investors

Makes companies disclose their real numbers, so ordinary people aren't cheated by insiders.

📋Licenses the players

Brokers, mutual funds, investment advisers and research analysts must be registered with SEBI.

⚖️Punishes fraud

Insider trading, price rigging and fake tips can lead to heavy fines and bans from the market.

🧭Writes the rules

From how IPOs work to how much margin F&O traders must put up, the rules come from SEBI.

If someone gives you investment advice for a fee, they must be a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser or Research Analyst. You can check any name in the intermediaries list on sebi.gov.in.
CHAPTER 2

The three accounts
you need.

To invest in shares you need three accounts working together. Most brokers open the last two for you in one go.

Bank account holds your money. Trading account (with a broker) is what you use to place orders. Demat account ("dematerialised") holds your shares electronically, like a bank account for shares. It sits at a depository, CDSL or NSDL, not with your broker, so your shares stay safe even if the broker shuts down.

CHAPTER 3

Opening an account:
KYC in 5 steps.

KYC means "Know Your Customer". It's how SEBI makes sure every investor is a real, verified person, which blocks money laundering and fraud. With most brokers it now takes about 15 minutes on your phone.

PAN cardMandatory. Every investment is linked to your PAN for tax.
AadhaarFor address proof and e-signing, linked to your mobile number for OTP.
Bank proofA cancelled cheque or bank statement, so money only flows to your own account.
Selfie or video verificationA quick live check that you are really you.
NomineeThe person who receives your investments if something happens to you.
CHAPTER 4

T+1: when shares
become yours.

"T" is the trade day. India moved fully to T+1 settlement in January 2023, one of the fastest systems in the world. Shares you buy land in your demat account on the next working day, and money from a sale reaches you the next working day too.

CHAPTER 5

Spot the scam.

Fake "tips" groups, guaranteed-return schemes and fake broker apps cost Indians crores every year. Read each message and decide: scam or legit?

LAB · SCAM OR LEGIT?
Golden rules: nobody can guarantee returns in the stock market. Never share an OTP or password. Only trade on apps from SEBI-registered brokers. If you are wronged, you can complain to SEBI through its SCORES portal.
