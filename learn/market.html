CHAPTER 1

A marketplace,
but for companies.

Think of your local vegetable market. Sellers bring tomatoes, buyers walk around, and a price is agreed for every sale. If tomatoes are scarce and everyone wants them, the price goes up.

The stock market works the same way, except what's being bought and sold are shares, which are small pieces of ownership in companies like Reliance, TCS or HDFC Bank.

India has two main stock exchanges: the NSE (National Stock Exchange) and the BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange, Asia's oldest, founded in 1875). They don't own the shares or set prices. Their job is to match buyers with sellers fairly, instantly, and under strict rules.

Key idea: when you buy a share, you're not buying it from the company or the exchange. You're buying it from another investor who wants to sell at that moment.

CHAPTER 3

The order book:
where prices are born.

Every stock has an order book, a live list of everyone waiting to buy (bids) and everyone waiting to sell (asks). A trade only happens when a buyer's price meets a seller's price.

There are two kinds of order. A market order says "buy now at whatever the best price is". A limit order says "buy only if I can get this price or better", and it waits in the book until someone matches it.

CHAPTER 4

Why prices go
up and down.

Nobody "sets" a share price. It moves because of a tug of war between buyers and sellers. When more people want to buy than sell, buyers have to offer more, so the price rises. When sellers outnumber buyers, it falls.

News changes that balance. Strong quarterly results bring buyers in. A scandal sends them running. Over the long run, prices tend to follow how much profit a company actually makes.

CHAPTER 5

Nifty and Sensex:
the market's pulse.

With thousands of listed companies, how do you know if "the market" went up today? You look at an index.

The Nifty 50 tracks 50 of the largest companies on the NSE. The Sensex tracks 30 large companies on the BSE. Bigger companies count for more. When the news says "Nifty fell 1%", it means those 50 big companies, taken together, lost about 1% of their value that day.

CHAPTER 6

Words you'll hear
every day.

🐂 Bull market

Prices are rising and people are optimistic. Bulls attack by thrusting their horns up.

🐻 Bear market

Prices are falling, usually 20% or more from a peak. Bears swipe their paws down.

LTP

Last Traded Price. The price of the most recent trade, which is what your app shows as the "price".

Bid / Ask

The best price buyers will pay, and the best price sellers will accept. The gap is the spread.

Volume

How many shares changed hands. High volume means lots of interest.

Market hours

NSE and BSE trade Monday to Friday, 9:15 am to 3:30 pm, with a pre-open session from 9:00 am.
